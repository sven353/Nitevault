Drink spiking can happen to anyone, regardless of gender, age, or what they are drinking. It can involve alcohol or drugs being added to a drink without someone's knowledge or consent, meaning even water or a soft drink can be spiked.

Women are disproportionately affected, but men can be victims too. UK surveys suggest that around 1 in 10 women and 1 in 20 men report having experienced drink spiking. And the true number is likely difficult to know because most incidents are never reported: recent research suggests that around 90% of suspected drink-spiking incidents are not reported to the police.

It also isn't necessarily a stranger who does it. A person responsible may be someone the victim knows or trusts.

The bottom line: you don't have to be drinking alcohol, and you don't have to be alone, to be at risk.
